# Clinical Guidelines: Pleural Effusion

## Diagnosis Criteria
- Blunting of the costophrenic angle on chest X-ray
- Decreased breath sounds, dullness to percussion on examination
- Ultrasound confirmation of fluid in the pleural space

## Workup
- Diagnostic thoracentesis (if fluid is >10mm thick on decubitus film or ultrasound)
- Pleural fluid analysis: Light's criteria (to distinguish transudate vs exudate)
  - Protein pleural/serum ratio > 0.5
  - LDH pleural/serum ratio > 0.6
  - Pleural LDH > 2/3 upper limit of normal for serum

## First-Line Treatment
- Transudative (e.g., heart failure, cirrhosis): Treat the underlying condition (diuretics, optimization of medical therapy)
- Exudative (e.g., pneumonia, malignancy): Treat underlying cause (antibiotics, chemotherapy)
- Large or symptomatic effusions: Therapeutic thoracentesis

## Monitoring
- Repeat chest imaging after thoracentesis to check for pneumothorax and residual fluid
- Monitor hemodynamics

## Red Flags (Escalate Immediately)
- Suspected empyema (pus in pleural space)
- Massive effusion causing mediastinal shift and hemodynamic compromise

## Source
Based on BTS Guidelines for Investigation of Unilateral Pleural Effusion
