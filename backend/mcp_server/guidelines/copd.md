# Clinical Guidelines: COPD Exacerbation

## Diagnosis Criteria
- Acute worsening of respiratory symptoms
- Increased dyspnea, increased sputum volume, increased sputum purulence
- History of smoking or occupational exposure

## Severity Assessment
- Mild: Treated with short-acting bronchodilators (SABDs) only
- Moderate: Treated with SABDs plus antibiotics and/or oral corticosteroids
- Severe: Patient requires emergency room visit or hospitalization

## First-Line Treatment
- Bronchodilators: Short-acting beta2-agonists (e.g., Albuterol) with or without short-acting anticholinergics
- Corticosteroids: Prednisone 40 mg per day for 5 days
- Antibiotics (if increased sputum purulence): Macrolides, tetracyclines, or amoxicillin

## Monitoring
- ABG (Arterial Blood Gas) if SaO2 < 92%
- Sputum culture if frequent exacerbator

## Red Flags (Escalate Immediately)
- Severe dyspnea unresponsive to initial therapy
- New onset cyanosis or peripheral edema
- Worsening hypoxemia or hypercapnia

## Source
Based on GOLD Guidelines (Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease)
